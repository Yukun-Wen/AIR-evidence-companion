§ Quantitative evidence and supported decision claims — Decision benefit requires evaluation of a clinical policy
Observational human-AI studies characterize how scores enter clinical decisions. A ranking-concordance study relates embryologist experience and cohort size to agreement with a 3D-CNN score. MAIA prospectively evaluates a static-image ANN during routine multicentre single-embryo transfer, including cases selected according to AI despite embryologist disagreement. Selection is observational, with conflicting reported subgroup counts. The studies describe agreement, adoption and disagreement resolution in practice, complementing intervention comparisons of the effect of assistance on outcomes [@EE004,EE012].
Adjudicated scope: AI adjudication: include_core. The paper directly evaluates a learned 3D-CNN ranking system on human IVF/ICSI embryos, with clinical fetal-heartbeat labels and manual-ranking comparisons. Retrospective evaluation of an existing model is empirical core evidence. Its study design cannot establish the benefit of providing AI assistance, despite the title and speculative conclusion.
Method summary: Prediction/ranking time: retrospective use of time-lapse frames from 20 hours post-insemination until embryo removal, with the specified model capable of using 20-148 hours; blastocysts were clinically graded at 116 or 140 hours. Biological units: embryo scores nested within usable euploid-embryo cohorts, treatment cycles and patients; primary concordance and fetal-heartbeat analyses were at the treatment/transferred-blastocyst level. Cohort/split: 1,321 eligible treatments from 1,165 patients at one UCSF site; this is an evaluation cohort, not a newly split training cohort, because the proprietary model had previously been trained on 181,429 sequences from 22 clinics. Labels/endpoints: manual top-ranked transferred euploid embryo, AI top rank, and fetal heartbeat at gestational week 6. Model: iDAScore v2, a 3D CNN. Comparator: blinded routine morphology-based selection and embryologist experience strata; concordant versus discordant selections. Outcomes: concordance and AUC are technical/decision outcomes; fetal heartbeat is clinical but the study did not prospectively assign selection by AI.

Source-linked evidence excerpts

[EE004-B0009] The retrospective single-centre cohort comprised PGT-A cycles cultured in EmbryoScope+ from February 2020 to October 2023; selection for transfer used morphology and euploid status while embryologists were blinded to AI scores.
[EE004-B0013] iDAScore v2 is a proprietary 3D convolutional neural network using 128 images from 20 to 148 hours post-insemination to output a 1.0-9.9 score associated with fetal-heartbeat likelihood.
[EE004-B0018] The final analysis included 1,321 treatments from 1,165 patients, 19,962 embryos and 20 embryologists across three experience categories.
[EE004-B0020] Among 1,321 transferred single euploid blastocysts, the score's fetal-heartbeat discrimination was AUC 0.669 (95% CI 0.637-0.700).
[EE004-B0021] Overall manual-AI top-choice concordance was 58.6% and rose from 51.3% for junior to 63.4% for Senior+ embryologists; adjusted contrasts were significant for Senior+ versus the other two groups.
[EE004-B0032] The authors identify PGT-A restriction, one site, unmeasured laboratory tasks and human factors, sex-selection effects, nonstandard experience categories, changing experience over time and strong confounding by cohort size as limitations.
