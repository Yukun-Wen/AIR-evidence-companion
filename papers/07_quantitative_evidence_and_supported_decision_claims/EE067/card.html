§ Quantitative evidence and supported decision claims — Decision benefit requires evaluation of a clinical policy
A paired-image experiment tests AI highlighting among euploid embryos with known implantation outcomes. Repeated assessment changes embryologists' choices across participating clinics. The design measures reader response to the display, while memory, presentation order and anchoring provide competing explanations for the observed change. Counterbalanced presentation and separate patient validation address these interaction and transport questions [@EE067].
Adjudicated scope: Empirical evaluation of learned AI assistance in selecting human IVF embryos is core evidence; a retrospective reader experiment is eligible without proving clinical benefit.
Method summary: Two hundred matched pairs of Day-5 euploid embryo images from 160 patients were presented to embryologists; each pair contained one embryo that implanted and one that did not. Fourteen embryologists from five centers completed baseline repeats and a subsequent AI-assisted assessment. The previously trained Xception-based model highlighted the embryo it ranked as more likely to implant, while embryologists remained blinded to the numerical score.

Source-linked evidence excerpts

EE067-H0125 The experiment used 200 pairs of Day-5 euploid embryos with known outcomes and asked embryologists to choose before and after AI identification of the higher-ranked image.
EE067-H0151 The same sets were repeated one year later and then again seven days later with reordered images and AI highlighting, creating a pre-post repeated-exposure design.
EE067-H0225 The AI selected the implanted embryo in 78.5% of pairs and embryologists improved from 65.5% to 73.6% after seeing the AI choice.
EE067-H0245 At least 75% embryologist consensus occurred in 62% of pairs before AI and 74% after AI.
