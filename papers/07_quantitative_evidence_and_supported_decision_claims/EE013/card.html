§ Quantitative evidence and supported decision claims — Labels and denominators determine what a metric measures
Training reproducibility provides another measurable property of a selection model. A CNN study varies random seeds and finds that similar aggregate discrimination can coexist with different within-patient rankings, including at a separate centre. This experiment measures the stability of proposed ordering under repeated fitting. Reporting rank agreement alongside performance uncertainty exposes variability in the decision that users receive [@EE013].

Adjudicated scope: AI adjudication: include_core. This is an original empirical evaluation of learned CNN stability on human IVF embryo datasets, not merely an opinion or general-method paper. Replicate-seed ranking and external-centre tests directly examine embryo-selection behavior. Negative/limited predictive performance does not make the study ineligible.
Method summary: Prediction time: static day-5 embryo image at 110 plus or minus 3 hours post-insemination. Biological units: embryo image nested in patient cohort; 10,713 MGH images/1,258 patients and 648 Cornell images/53 patients, with ranking subsets of 92 and 49 patients and critical-error subsets of 83 and 45. Cohort/split: MGH data were used for training/validation and an MGH test set; Cornell was a fully separate external cohort with no pooling or retraining. Exact training/validation/test counts and grouping are referred to supplements, while the main results mention 191 validation embryos and 172 test embryos. Labels: live birth for transferred embryos; nontransferred embryos lacked observed clinical outcomes; retrospective Gardner-style grades were used only to define critical errors. Model: 50 conventional single-instance CNN replicates differing only in random seed, with softmax live-birth scores used for ranking. Comparators: replicate seeds, alternative training/pretraining settings and internal versus external centre; no standard-of-care efficacy arm. Outcomes: individual prediction metrics, Kendall rank stability, clinician-choice transfer alignment, conditional live-birth rates and critical top-rank errors; this is a retrospective reliability analysis, not evidence of AI-guided clinical benefit.

Source-linked evidence excerpts

[EE013-B0005] The primary data included 10,713 day-5 embryo images from 1,258 MGH patients, captured at 110 plus or minus 3 hours post-insemination.
[EE013-B0006] An independent Cornell dataset contained 648 images from 53 patients; centres remained separate and Cornell was evaluated without retraining.
[EE013-B0008] Fifty replicate models shared architecture and training data but varied random seed; within-patient embryo rankings were evaluated for 92 MGH and 49 Cornell patients with at least four embryos.
[EE013-B0016] Across the 50 models and 172-embryo test set, mean AUC was 60.02% with range 49.47%-71.78%, alongside wide variability in other metrics.
[EE013-B0017] Mean rank concordance across replicate models was low: Kendall W 0.3571 at MGH and 0.3410 at Cornell.
[EE013-B0018] A degenerate embryo was top-ranked despite an available blastocyst in a mean 12.41% of eligible MGH cohorts and 17.29% of Cornell cohorts, with broad seed-to-seed ranges.
[EE013-B0023] The authors emphasize that no definitive ground-truth total ranking exists and that conventional AUC/accuracy gives limited guidance for the clinically important ranking task.
