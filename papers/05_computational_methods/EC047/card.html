§ Computational methods — Trajectory summaries and recorded-action learning
Changes, extrema and slopes compress serial observations into fixed trajectory summaries, complementing Section 5.3's full-sequence encoders [@R045]. Scan-day-specific models use evolving follicular measurements to predict clinician-selected trigger timing and a high-response proxy. Validation groups scans by cycle, with repeated-woman separation unspecified and eligibility changing by scan day. These outputs characterize prescribing patterns and proxy response at successive monitoring visits [@EC047].
Adjudicated scope: Empirical human IVF/ICSI monitoring models satisfy core clinical-operations scope. The work contains both clinician-timing imitation and high-response prediction, not a tested reduced-scan care pathway.
Method summary: Prediction time varies by scan day, from baseline through stimulation days 3-9. Units are scans nested within cycles and women: 9,294 scans, 2,322 total cycles, 1,875 women; 2,128 cycles from 1,731 patients were complete after exclusions. Endpoints were (1) the observed clinician-selected trigger day as a continuous regression target and (2) binary high response, defined by >18 follicles >=11 mm on trigger day and/or >=18 oocytes collected. Age missingness was <1% and was mean-imputed within cross-validation folds; AMH was missing for a substantial fraction and excluded. Fivefold cross-validation was performed at cycle level, not stated to be grouped by woman. Trigger-day random forest was compared with mean historical trigger day; the risk classifier had no clinical benchmark beyond performance across scan days. Trigger timing is prescriptive agreement with historical clinician decisions, while high response is learned response prediction. Neither establishes that reducing scans or following model timing causes equal or better outcomes.
