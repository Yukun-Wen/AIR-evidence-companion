§ Computational methods — Direct video encoders and shared representations
Forecasting future frames makes an image sequence the prediction target. An exploratory ConvLSTM study trains separate models using retrospective transfer and discard strata, then evaluates synthesized development. Its image-fidelity metrics assess the forecast appearance within those strata. Recursive forecasting extends a next-frame model over several steps; seven frames span different biological durations at different acquisition rates. Forecast evaluation therefore requires both the prediction horizon and elapsed time [@EE011].
Adjudicated scope: AI adjudication: include_core. Learned forecasting is evaluated on real human IVF embryo videos, directly addressing laboratory assessment. Technical generation of future images satisfies the empirical criterion without reproductive outcome evaluation. Retrospective strata and image quality limitations restrict the permitted interpretation.
Method summary: Prediction times: models use seven recent frames (about two hours) to predict the next frame or recursively forecast future development in two windows, 31-43 hours and 90-113 hours post-insemination; claims of 12-23-hour forecasting refer to recursive horizons, while the updating seven-frame strategy looks roughly two hours ahead. Biological unit: embryo time-lapse video/frame sequence; 365 videos, with patient counts and repeated embryos per patient not reported. Cohort/split: one Norwegian clinic; separate transfer and discard datasets, development/validation splits within model-training videos, plus small independent video sets (10/10 for cleavage transfer/avoid, 7/8 for blastocyst transfer/avoid). Labels/targets: future observed frames and embryologist-annotated time/cell stage; 'transfer' and 'avoid' are pre-existing strata used to train different models rather than classes predicted by one model. Model: four-layer ConvLSTM FramePredictor, plus a separately trained U-Net cropper and two recursive forecasting strategies. Comparators: the two forecasting strategies and pre/post cropper results; no conventional clinical selection or outcome-prediction comparator. Outcomes: PSNR, SSIM, FVD and qualitative embryologist assessment are technical outputs; no implantation, pregnancy or live-birth outcome.

Source-linked evidence excerpts

[EE011-B0012] The dataset comprised 365 low-fragmentation embryo videos: 220 covering 31-43 hours post-insemination and 145 covering 90-113 hours.
[EE011-B0025] Four separate ConvLSTM FramePredictor models were trained for transfer versus avoid videos and for cleavage versus blastocyst-stage windows.
[EE011-B0027] For the cleavage-stage study, each category used 100 videos for model development and 10 independent evaluation videos.
[EE011-B0046] Single-next-frame evaluation reported independent post-cropping PSNR/SSIM of 25.84/0.93 for cleavage-transfer videos and 26.59/0.95 for blastocyst-transfer videos, with worse blastocyst-avoid generalization.
[EE011-B0070] Recursive seven-frame forecasts had grayscale distortions, blurred membranes and a mean 1-1.5-hour delay in stage transitions.
[EE011-B0072] Embryologists could observe developmental changes and blastulation onset, but judged noise-free predictions necessary for clinical validation.
[EE011-B0080] The authors attribute poor generalization for blastocyst-stage avoid videos to overfitting and limited eligible data.
