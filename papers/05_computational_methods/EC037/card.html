§ Computational methods — Nonlinear estimators and response targets
Genetic inputs extend clinical-history representations. One study predicts MII-oocyte counts from clinical-genetic features and previous-cycle outcomes; extensive variant searches, repeated cycles and incompletely specified nesting leave selection optimism unresolved. A donor study classifies total-oocyte response using baseline, genetic and realized treatment variables in selected young donors, excluding very low responses. SHAP identifies predictive variants within this population. The studies share a pharmacogenetic motivation while defining different targets, information sets and generalization populations [@EC053,EC037].
Adjudicated scope: Human oocyte-donor stimulation with empirical learned three-class response modeling meets core eligibility. The study is chiefly exploratory pharmacogenetic association and internal prediction under observed treatment; selected donors, truncated low responses and late treatment inputs restrict interpretation, not scope.
Method summary: Prediction/assessment time is not a clean pretreatment deployment point: the model includes total gonadotropin dose, protocol, and stimulation duration observed during the completed cycle, alongside baseline donor variables and genotypes. Unit: one first donor stimulation per woman (495 women/cycles). Endpoint transformation: retrieved-oocyte count was converted into sub-optimal (4-9), normal (10-20), and hyper-response (>20) classes; cancelled cycles and counts <4 were excluded. Missingness: 0.06% was reported and imputed with MICE/CART. Five classifiers were compared after an 80/20 random split, class balancing, preprocessing, and fivefold cross-validation for hyperparameter adjustment; the reported final metrics were on the 20% test set. The comparator set was multilayer perceptron, radial-basis SVM, k-nearest neighbors, random forest, and XGBoost. This is learned response classification/association under observed stimulation, not a test of genotype-guided dosing or causal policy benefit.
