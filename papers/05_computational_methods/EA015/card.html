§ Computational methods — Monitoring sequences and treatment feedback
ILETIA combines transformer embeddings with boosted trees to estimate trigger-to-retrieval intervals. Its labels are observed timings in cycles already meeting a retrieval-rate criterion. Temporal testing evaluates reproduction of the timing patterns in those selected cycles, giving the learned interval a clear historical reference. Applying the interval as a recommendation defines a subsequent intervention with retrieval outcomes as the relevant endpoint [@EA015].
Adjudicated scope: AI adjudication. Learned interval and ovulation classifiers/regressors are developed and evaluated on human PPOS records with a later temporal test. The outcome-conditioned interval target remains a predictive historical label rather than proof of optimal intervention timing.
Method summary: A single-center PPOS dataset comprised 8,730 patients. The main interval dataset retained 8,657 cycles with oocyte-pickup rate at least 0.75 and intervals from 2,100 to 2,280 minutes; 8,383 older cases formed training data and 274 later cases a temporal test set. FT-transformer embeddings were passed to XGBoost for three-class interval classification and LightGBM for regression. A premature-ovulation model used 95 events, selected low-risk controls, CTGAN-generated positives, and XGBoost. Comparisons included conventional and deep tabular baselines, clinician ratings, subgroup analyses, and four additional stimulation protocols.
