§ Computational methods — Early, intermediate and late fusion
Uterine imaging provides complementary fusion designs. An endometrial study clusters ultrasound regions into habitats, derives radiomic features and combines them with clinical variables for clinical-pregnancy prediction. This structure introduces imaging information and regional organization as two components. Comparisons with clinical-only and whole-region models can separate their contributions. A prospective first-IVF/ICSI cohort uses expert MUSA ultrasound features in a pretreatment cumulative-live-birth model; adding embryo stage and frozen-transfer information post hoc yields a later-information predictor [@EC057,EC058].
Adjudicated scope: AI adjudication: The empirical model combines endometrial ultrasound radiomics and clinical inputs in human IVF/ICSI transfer patients to predict clinical pregnancy. This directly meets transfer/outcome core scope; the study does not establish causal treatment benefit or external transportability.
Method summary: Retrospective single-center cohort of 543 transfer patients was randomly split 380/163. Manually segmented endometrial ultrasound ROIs were partitioned into four K-means habitats; 7,805 radiomic features were reduced to 15 and combined with clinical predictors across 11 algorithms. Grid-searched five-fold cross-validation selected models, and SHAP described feature contributions.
