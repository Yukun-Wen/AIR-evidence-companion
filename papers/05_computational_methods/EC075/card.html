§ Computational methods — Early, intermediate and late fusion
Ultrasound timing locates the target population within pregnancy. Gestational-sac and embryonic-bud radiomics combined with clinical variables predict live birth after ultrasound confirmation of a viable singleton pregnancy four weeks after blastocyst transfer [@EC059]. A delta-radiomics study uses changes between week-six and week-eight scans in selected viable singleton pregnancies. Its combined model exceeds the clinical comparator's reported test AUC, while delta features alone do not. Both designs use post-implantation information for prognosis among pregnancies meeting the imaging criteria [@EC075].
Adjudicated scope: AI adjudication: human SVBT post-implantation radiomics with empirically compared learned classifiers is core eligible even though a conventional logistic signature is retained. Prediction uses week-6 and week-8 ultrasound in selected viable singleton intrauterine pregnancies.
Method summary: A retrospective single-centre cohort of 528 singleton intrauterine pregnancies after SVBT was split 70/30. Gestational-sac radiomics at weeks 6 and 8 produced 1561 features per time point; delta concatenation produced 3122 features. Univariate filtering, correlation pruning, LASSO, ten algorithm comparisons, age integration, and SHAP interpretation were applied.
