§ Introduction
Three recent reviews provide particularly close precedents. Lorimer and colleagues connect AI to laboratory automation, distinguish embryo ranking from competency prediction, and examine clinical utility. Hu and colleagues synthesize clinical applications with explicit attention to patient-level partitions, internal and external validation, and patient benefit. Ouyang and Wei's formal review organizes embryo methods by data modality, catalogues public datasets, and summarizes performance and technology-use frequencies [@R014,R015,R028]. Lorimer and colleagues also describe the iDAScore AUC contrast introduced above and the gains from adding clinical metadata to IVFormer [@R014]. Building on these accounts, our synthesis retains the exact inputs, label policies, evaluation populations and biological units of each contrast, and implements explicit counting rules for resource reuse and model families.

§ Computational methods — Multimodal and hierarchical representations
Patient context, microscopy, developmental trajectories, ultrasound and molecular measurements describe complementary aspects of treatment. Broad IVF reviews and Ouyang and Wei's formal review organize these inputs and their use in embryo grading and pregnancy prediction [@R005,R012,R028]. Multimodal design determines where information from these sources interacts; hierarchical design determines how observations from several biological objects contribute to one output. Both begin by aligning the observations to their biological units and acquisition times.
